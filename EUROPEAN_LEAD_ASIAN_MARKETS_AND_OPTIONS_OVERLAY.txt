ASIAN MARKETS + OPTIONS OVERLAY -- EXTENSIONS TO THE EUROPEAN LEAD SIGNAL
=============================================================================
2026-07-23, research branch. Two follow-ups to
EUROPEAN_LEAD_US_FIRST_HOUR_BACKTEST.txt: does the same lead-lag logic
extend to Asian markets (which finish their whole session even earlier
than Europe opens), and can the DAX-direction signal be expressed via
SPY/QQQ options instead of shares for more leverage.

PART 1: ASIAN MARKETS -- A REAL BUT OPPOSITE-SIGN EFFECT
-------------------------------------------------------------
Nikkei 225, Hang Seng, and Shanghai Composite all finish their full
session (open to close) well before Europe even opens, let alone before
the US -- unlike Europe's partial-session-before-cutoff signal, these
use the whole day's return.

Individual correlation with SPY's first hour (2bp-cost-equivalent
sample, ~440-450 days):

  Symbol               Direction corr   Direction pctile   Magnitude corr   Magnitude pctile
  Nikkei 225            -0.090            2.4th              0.229            100.0
  Hang Seng              -0.086            3.6th              0.143             99.8
  Shanghai Composite     -0.104            1.8th              0.103             98.2

Every one of these is statistically real (shuffle-control percentiles
confirm it isn't noise) -- but DIRECTION IS NEGATIVE, the opposite sign
from DAX's +0.277. A big up-day in Asia weakly precedes a DOWN move in
the US first hour, not an up move. Magnitude stays positive across all
three, same as Europe -- size of the Asian move still predicts size of
the US first-hour move, just not which way.

Naively averaging Asia + Europe into one combined signal cancels the
directional components out (combined dir_corr -0.019, not significant,
34.9th percentile) and the resulting backtest is flat-to-negative
(-0.58bps/trade every day, -2.11bps/trade top-quartile at 2bp cost).
Averaging the two regions is the wrong way to combine them.

BETTER COMBINATION: use Asia's magnitude (real signal) as a conviction
filter on DAX's direction (real signal), without importing Asia's
contrarian direction:

  DAX-direction-only:                     win 53.0%, mean 3.64bps, Sharpe 1.76
  DAX-direction, gated by Asia magnitude:  win 57.4%, mean 5.75bps, Sharpe 2.56
  (gated on Asia's move being above its own median; trade count roughly halves)

This is a real, meaningful improvement -- Sharpe rises from 1.76 to 2.56
by using Asia only as a "something is moving today" filter, not a
direction vote. Read Asia's contrarian direction finding as a genuinely
interesting open question, not solved here: plausibly overnight moves
that occurred many hours before US open have more time to partially
mean-revert than same-morning European moves do, but that's a
hypothesis, not something tested directly.

PART 2: OPTIONS OVERLAY (SPY/QQQ, SAME-DAY 0DTE-STYLE)
------------------------------------------------------------
Expresses the DAX-direction signal via a same-day call (signal positive)
or put (signal negative), ATM strike, bought at US open and sold an hour
later -- SPY and QQQ both have real daily option expirations, so this is
a realistic structure, not a contrived one.

IMPORTANT NEW CAVEAT, on top of every other options caveat in this
project: 0DTE Black-Scholes-off-realized-vol pricing is the LEAST
reliable pricing regime tested here. Real 0DTE dynamics (extreme gamma,
dealer hedging flows, pinning toward round strikes near expiry) are not
well captured by Black-Scholes at all -- BS assumes smooth continuous
price evolution and constant vol, exactly what 0DTE options violate
hardest. Treat everything below as a generous upper-bound sketch of what
leverage COULD do to this edge, not a real backtest of a real 0DTE
strategy.

Priced with years-to-expiry as a FRACTION OF A DAY (6.5 hours to close
at entry, 5.5 remaining at the 1-hour exit) and realized vol from
trailing HOURLY bars (not daily closes), annualized via
sqrt(252 x 6.5 trading hours), not sqrt(252).

  SPY 0DTE, 5% spread:  462 trades, win 48.1%, mean +37.3%/trade, PF 2.27, maxDD 19.6%, total return +1724%
  SPY 0DTE, 10% spread: 462 trades, win 45.5%, mean +24.2%/trade, PF 1.76, maxDD 22.8%, total return +1117%
  QQQ 0DTE, 5% spread:  462 trades, win 44.6%, mean +37.5%/trade, PF 2.09, maxDD 17.0%, total return +1733%
  QQQ 0DTE, 10% spread: 462 trades, win 42.6%, mean +24.3%/trade, PF 1.67, maxDD 25.3%, total return +1125%

Checked the raw per-trade numbers directly before trusting this (same
process that caught the tail-hedge and debit-spread bugs earlier
tonight) -- entry/exit premiums land in a realistic dollar range
($0.22-$8.77 on a SPY-scale underlying), and the shape is qualitatively
correct for real 0DTE behavior: losers decay to near-zero within the
hour (theta burn is genuinely that fast this close to expiry) while
winners can run several hundred percent on a leveraged gamma move. Win
rate is LOWER than the underlying equity signal (48% vs. the equity
strategy's 53%) because being directionally right isn't enough for a
0DTE option -- the move has to be big enough, fast enough, to overcome
theta within a single hour. The positive expectancy comes entirely from
payoff asymmetry (capped losses near -100%, occasional 500-700% winners),
not from a high hit rate.

Read the "+1724% total return" number as a qualitative confirmation that
leverage amplifies a real directional edge (expected), not as a real
return estimate -- real 0DTE bid-ask spreads (which can be wide as a %
of premium away from the most liquid at-the-money strikes, especially
in the first minutes of trading), actual fill slippage, and pin/
assignment risk near the money would all eat into this, likely
substantially, in ways this model cannot honestly estimate without real
quote data.

VERDICT
--------
Asian markets add a real, usable refinement (magnitude-gating) once
combined correctly -- worth keeping. The contrarian directional
finding is a genuinely interesting open question for a future session,
not something to trade on its own without understanding the mechanism
better. The options overlay confirms the directional edge is real
enough to survive leveraging, which is useful signal, but the specific
numbers here are the least trustworthy of anything produced tonight --
0DTE pricing without real quotes is closer to a sanity check than a
strategy proposal. If this whole thread gets pursued toward paper
trading, the equity version (real SPY/shares, real cost model already
validated) is the one to actually deploy; the options version needs
real 0DTE quote data before it's more than a "the leverage direction
makes sense" confirmation.
