EUROPEAN LEAD SIGNAL -> US FIRST HOUR: FULL BACKTEST
========================================================
2026-07-23, research branch. Turns the correlational finding in
EUROPEAN_LEAD_US_FIRST_HOUR_STUDY.txt into an actual backtest: real
entry/exit rules, cost sensitivity, out-of-sample validation,
individual-stock extension, and a macro-event-day check -- the four
things flagged as missing when the raw correlation was first reported.

STRATEGY
---------
At US open (13:30 UTC), go long SPY if that morning's DAX pre-open
return (DAX session open through the last DAX bar before 13:30 UTC --
strictly no lookahead) was positive, short if negative, flat if exactly
zero. Exit at the end of the first hour (14:30 UTC) -- the same bar the
correlational study measured, so this can't quietly redefine the signal
to look better after the fact. Trades never overlap (same-day open and
close), so a plain daily return series and honest compounding equity
curve are both valid -- this doesn't need the overlap-safe single-
position machinery the multi-day equity studies required.

Two variants tested throughout: TRADE EVERY DAY, and TRADE ONLY THE TOP
QUARTILE BY |DAX MOVE| (the subset where the correlational study found
the effect concentrated).

COST MODEL AND ITS LIMITS
----------------------------
No real historical SPY quote (bid/ask) data is available for free --
same constraint as every cost assumption in this project. SPY is one of
the most liquid instruments that exists, so a tight assumption is
defensible, but tested across 1/2/5 bps round-trip rather than picking
one favorable number. The open print specifically has a wider effective
spread than mid-day (price discovery) -- the higher end of the range is
meant to cover that, not just general market-making spread.

RESULTS: COST SENSITIVITY (462 trading days, 2023-09-06 to 2026-07-23)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
                          1 bp cost              2 bp cost              5 bp cost (stress)
                    win%   mean    Sharpe   win%   mean    Sharpe   win%   mean    Sharpe
Trade every day     53.7%  4.65bps  2.19    52.2%  3.65bps  1.72    47.8%  0.65bps  0.31
Top quartile only    61.2% 10.57bps 4.16    61.2%  9.57bps  3.76    57.8%  6.57bps  2.58
Always-long (context) 52.8% 0.21bps 0.10    51.5% -0.79bps -0.37    45.9% -3.79bps -1.76

"Always-long" is SPY's own raw first-hour drift with no signal at all --
included as context. It's near-zero/negative at every cost level, which
matters: the edge above is NOT just "SPY tends to rise in the first
hour and any long-biased rule looks good." It comes specifically from
following the DAX-conditioned direction.

Random-direction control (1,000 reshuffled seeds, same trade count/
timing, real DAX-signal mean vs. that distribution):
  1 bp cost: real=4.65bps vs random mean=-0.87bps (std 1.56) -> 99.9th percentile
  2 bp cost: real=3.65bps vs random mean=-1.87bps (std 1.56) -> 99.9th percentile
  5 bp cost: real=0.65bps vs random mean=-4.87bps (std 1.56) -> 99.9th percentile
Consistently 99.9th percentile at every cost level tested -- not
sensitive to the specific cost assumption.

OUT-OF-SAMPLE SPLIT (the check that matters most given how strong this looks)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sharpe ratios above 2 on ~2 years of data are exactly the kind of number
that should trigger real skepticism about overfitting. Split the sample
at the midpoint and re-ran independently on each half:

                        First half (2023-09 to 2025-04, n=232)   Second half (2025-04 to 2026-07, n=230)
Every day, 2bp cost:    win 53.9%, mean 4.64bps, Sharpe 1.97      win 50.4%, mean 2.66bps, Sharpe 1.43
Top quartile, 2bp cost: win 60.3%, mean 10.67bps, Sharpe 3.48     win 62.1%, mean 8.87bps, Sharpe 4.73

The effect holds in BOTH halves, at similar magnitude, and the
top-quartile variant is actually slightly STRONGER in the more recent
half (Sharpe 4.73 vs 3.48) rather than decaying -- the opposite of what
a spurious/overfit in-sample artifact would typically show. This is the
single most reassuring check run on this signal.

INDIVIDUAL STOCKS: THE EFFECT DOES NOT TRANSFER
----------------------------------------------------
Same signal and backtest applied to AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA (2bp cost):

Symbol   Direction corr   Every day (pctile vs random)      Top quartile
AAPL     0.061            win 46.8%, mean -2.83bps (45th)    win 47.4%, mean  2.21bps, Sharpe 0.28
MSFT     0.067            win 48.7%, mean -0.45bps (64th)    win 59.5%, mean  6.78bps, Sharpe 1.25
NVDA     0.111            win 51.9%, mean  0.08bps (59th)    win 61.2%, mean 14.76bps, Sharpe 1.32
TSLA     0.106            win 53.7%, mean 18.95bps (99th)    win 56.0%, mean 23.13bps, Sharpe 1.77

Only TSLA shows a real every-day effect (99th percentile); AAPL shows
none at all; MSFT and NVDA only show something in the filtered
top-quartile subset, and more weakly than SPY. Read this as a feature
of the finding, not a weakness in it: it says the mechanism is
genuinely about BROAD MARKET / macro risk sentiment crossing time
zones (which hits a market-cap-weighted index cleanly) rather than
stock-specific information leakage (which would show up evenly across
individual names, or concentrate on the most "European-exposed"
individual stocks specifically -- it doesn't). TSLA's partial showing
fits this too -- it's a well-known high-beta, sentiment-driven name,
closer to a risk-on/risk-off proxy than a fundamentals-driven stock.

DOES IT CONCENTRATE AROUND FOMC DAYS? NO.
---------------------------------------------
Checked whether the edge is really just "FOMC-day positioning" in
disguise -- compared FOMC decision days (15 in the sample window, second
day of each 2-day meeting, 2023-09 through 2026-06) against all other
days, 2bp cost:

  FOMC decision days (n=15):  win 53.3%, mean 1.37bps, Sharpe 0.79
  Non-FOMC days (n=447):      win 52.1%, mean 3.73bps, Sharpe 1.75

If anything the effect is WEAKER on FOMC days (though n=15 is too small
to read much into that specific gap). The honest conclusion: this is a
general daily phenomenon, not scheduled-event-anticipation trading in
disguise -- which is actually the cleaner, more useful result
operationally (the signal doesn't need special-casing around a macro
calendar). ECB meeting dates weren't checked with the same precision
(couldn't get a complete verified date list) -- a real gap, not
concealed.

VERDICT
--------
This is the most credible finding to come out of this project's options/
signal research tonight. It clears every bar this project holds other
candidates to: real out-of-sample split (holds, even strengthens),
random-shuffle control (99.9th percentile, every cost level), a
sensible economic mechanism (index-level macro sentiment has to
physically cross time zones) rather than a backtest-shaped
after-the-fact story, and it survives a stress-level 5bp cost
assumption in the top-quartile variant (Sharpe 2.58).

What's still missing before this is promotion-grade, stated plainly:
real SPY bid/ask quote data (not an assumption, however defensible);
real execution modeling (slippage beyond spread, especially at the
open specifically); a live paper-trading test the way every other
candidate in this project has to earn its way in, not just a backtest;
and ideally a mechanistic explanation deeper than "macro sentiment"
(e.g. does it track VIX futures or overnight S&P futures moves too, or
is DAX specifically doing something those don't already capture).

Recommended next step: shadow-paper-test this the same way simple_trend
was proven out before going live -- log hypothetical same-day trades
against real intraday quotes for a few weeks before committing anything
resembling real logic to the live deployment path.
